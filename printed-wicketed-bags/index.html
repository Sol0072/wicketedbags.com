Buyer's guide

Custom printed wicketed bags: how it works.

Flexo printing, up to 8 colors

We print wicketed poly bags with flexographic (flexo) printing. Each color in your design gets its own printing plate, and we can run up to 8 colors. A one- or two-color logo is simple and clean. A full-color design with photos and gradients uses more colors. Both work on PE and PP film.

What to send for artwork

  • Vector files if you have them (AI, EPS or PDF), with fonts converted to outlines.
  • Your brand colors, as Pantone (PMS) numbers if you know them.
  • Any copy that must appear on the bag, exactly as it should read.

Only have a logo as an image file, or a bag you like? Send that. We'll tell you what we need.

Plan the layout around the wicket

A wicketed bag has a lip at the top with two wicket holes, and that lip stays behind when the bag is pulled off. Keep your print below the lip, and keep the important parts away from the seals and the edges. On clear bags, think about what shows through: many brands leave a clear window so the product does the talking.

Printed bags for every line

We print bags for bread and bakery, pita, tortillas, produce, frozen food and ice, and shirts and apparel, or see all our wicketed bags. Food bags are made from FDA-compliant films for food contact.

FAQ

Printed wicketed bag questions.

How many colors can you print on wicketed bags?

Up to 8 colors, flexo printed.

What films can you print on?

Clear, white and metallized films, in PE and PP.

How long do printed wicketed bags take?

Typical lead time is 3–4 weeks. Everything is printed and converted in house in Brooklyn, NY.

What artwork files should I send?

Vector files (AI, EPS or PDF) with fonts outlined are best, plus your Pantone colors if you know them. Only have an image or a sample bag? Send that and we'll tell you what we need.

Where should the print go on a wicketed bag?

Below the lip, since the lip with the wicket holes stays on the wicket when the bag is pulled off. Keep key text away from seals and edges.
